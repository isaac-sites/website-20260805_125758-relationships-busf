## Introduction

H. G. Wells’s *[The War]({{ 'the-war/' | relative_url }}) of the Worlds* helped create one of the most durable visual and technological templates for imagining visitors from space. The novel’s Martians were not simply alien beings; they were a civilisation defined by machines that seemed far beyond human capability. Their towering fighting machines, heat ray and interplanetary arrival system gave later generations a vocabulary for describing what an advanced extraterrestrial presence might look like.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_machine_%28The_War_of_the_Worlds%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span></span></span>

This influence did not mean that [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}) were caused by [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}), or that witnesses were consciously copying Wells. The connection was more subtle: fictional technology supplied familiar images and expectations that shaped how mysterious objects in the sky were described and interpreted. When “flying saucers” became a major cultural idea after 1947, the public was already accustomed to imagining spacecraft and alien devices through decades of science fiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/history-of-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">of UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of UFOs - Sightings, Timeline &amp; Abductions &#124; HISTORYOctober 27, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 27, 2009</span></span></span>

## The original invasion technology

### Wells made alien machines the centre of the encounter

In *The War of the Worlds* (1898), the Martians arrive through cylindrical spacecraft launched from Mars, but the machines that dominate the story are the fighting machines commonly known as tripods. These enormous walking vehicles combine mobility, weapons and mechanical superiority into a single image of extraterrestrial power. Their height, unusual shape and unfamiliar method of movement made them unlike ordinary human vehicles, reinforcing the idea that alien technology would not simply be an improved version of existing machines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_machine_%28The_War_of_the_Worlds%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span></span></span>

The most important feature of Wells’s technology was not its engineering realism but its narrative function. The heat ray represented a weapon based on a scientific principle beyond the understanding of the people facing it. The Martian machines could destroy human defences while remaining mysterious, creating a pattern later repeated in alien invasion fiction: advanced visitors are recognised through their technological advantage rather than through communication alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighting_machine_%28The_War_of_the_Worlds%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fighting machine (The War of the Worlds</span></span></span>

This idea became part of the cultural language of extraterrestrial contact. Before modern [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}) developed, Wells had already established several assumptions that would appear again in discussions of possible alien visitors:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Alien civilisations would be judged by their machines.
* Space travel would require technology dramatically superior to humanity’s.
* Strange objects from the sky could represent evidence of an advanced intelligence.
* The appearance of unfamiliar technology would be as important as the identity of its creators.

</div>

The lasting importance of Wells’s invention was therefore not a specific vehicle design but a mechanism of interpretation: unknown objects became easier to imagine as products of another civilisation’s technology.

## Spacecraft ideas in UFO culture

### From Martian machines to flying saucers

The emergence of the “flying saucer” after 1947 occurred in a world already filled with science fiction imagery. [Kenneth Arnold]({{ 'kenneth-arnold/' | relative_url }})’s widely reported sighting near Mount Rainier helped popularise the term after he described objects moving in an unusual way; newspapers later associated his report with the phrase “flying saucer”. The objects themselves were not originally described as literal saucers, but the phrase quickly became linked with the idea of alien spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/project-blue-book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book - Alien, Definition &amp; Files &#124; HISTORYFebruary 22, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

The shift from Wells’s tripods to saucer-shaped craft shows how [UFO imagery]({{ 'ufo-imagery/' | relative_url }}) evolved rather than simply copied earlier fiction. Wells imagined land-based war machines because late nineteenth-century audiences understood industrial power through armies, factories and mechanical vehicles. By the mid-twentieth century, aviation, rockets and space exploration changed expectations. The imagined alien machine moved from the battlefield to the sky.

Science fiction helped provide the transition. Popular stories, films and illustrations repeatedly presented extraterrestrial visitors arriving in advanced craft, making the idea of a mysterious object from space culturally familiar. When witnesses reported unusual lights or shapes in the sky, descriptions were often interpreted within a framework that already included spacecraft from fiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/57c6c3190457d5188d59745df2e0bd3c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">since World War II has been a blend of sightings, investigations, and cultural fascination. ### The technology gap became the key expectation

A major connection between Wells’s Martians and UFO culture was the assumption of technological imbalance. In *The War of the Worlds*, humans are not defeated because they lack courage; they are overwhelmed because the Martians possess superior tools. Later UFO narratives frequently used a similar logic: if an object was truly extraterrestrial, it would likely display abilities beyond known aircraft technology.

This expectation influenced how unusual movements, speed or shapes were discussed. Reports of objects that appeared to manoeuvre in unfamiliar ways were often connected to ideas of advanced propulsion or unknown engineering rather than simply being treated as unidentified observations. Official investigations such as the US Air Force’s Project Blue Book examined thousands of reports, while concluding that no investigated UFO provided evidence of extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104590/unidentified-flying-objects-and-air-force-project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The important cultural effect was not that fiction proved alien technology existed. Instead, fiction supplied a ready-made explanation for what unknown technology might mean.

## Fiction and real sightings

### How imagined machines shaped interpretation

The relationship between science fiction and UFO [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) is best understood as a feedback loop. Writers imagined possible alien technologies, those images entered popular culture, and later reports of unexplained phenomena were sometimes interpreted through those familiar images.

Wells’s machines contributed one part of that process by making advanced alien technology imaginable before humans had reached space. Later generations added new designs: smooth spacecraft, glowing propulsion systems, silent aircraft-like vehicles and other concepts drawn from changing ideas about aviation and space travel. UFO culture did not inherit a single machine from *The War of the Worlds*; it inherited the broader expectation that strange objects in the sky might represent the hardware of another civilisation.

This influence can be seen in the language used around UFOs. Terms such as “craft”, “technology” and “advanced vehicles” frame sightings as possible engineering problems rather than purely supernatural events. That approach reflects the scientific and technological atmosphere of the twentieth century, but it also owes something to earlier science fiction, where alien machines provided a bridge between imagination and speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/history-of-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">of UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of UFOs - Sightings, Timeline &amp; Abductions &#124; HISTORYOctober 27, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 27, 2009</span></span></span>

### The boundary between inspiration and evidence

The strongest claim that can be made is about cultural influence, not physical evidence. Wells’s Martian technology shaped expectations by giving people images and concepts for thinking about extraterrestrial visitors. It did not create proof of alien spacecraft, and official investigations have not established that UFO reports represent extraterrestrial machines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104590/unidentified-flying-objects-and-air-force-project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The significance of Wells’s inventions lies in how they changed the questions people asked. After *The War of the Worlds*, imagining visitors from another planet often meant imagining their machines first: the vehicles they would use, the weapons they might possess and the technological gap they might reveal. That legacy helped prepare the cultural ground on which modern UFO imagery developed.

    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
