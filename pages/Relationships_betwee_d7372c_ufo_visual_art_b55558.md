## Introduction

The most influential UFO images emerged during the mid-20th century, when reports of strange objects, the rise of science fiction magazines and the development of special-effects cinema combined to create a shared visual culture. From the polished discs of 1950s [invasion films]({{ 'invasion-films/' | relative_url }}) to the luminous spacecraft of *Close Encounters of the [Third Kind]({{ 'third-kind/' | relative_url }})*, UFO imagery has shifted from representing threat and invasion to representing mystery, communication and possible transformation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-and-alien-invasions-in-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY TV Nederland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TV Nederland UFOs and Alien Invasions in Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY TV NederlandUFOs and Alien Invasions in Film - Meaning, Hollywood, List &#124; HISTORYApril 9, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2010</span></span></span>

## How the flying saucer became a science fiction icon

The classic disc-shaped UFO is one of the clearest examples of an image moving between reported experience, popular culture and artistic invention. The phrase “flying saucer” became widely known after pilot [Kenneth Arnold]({{ 'kenneth-arnold/' | relative_url }})’s 1947 report of unusual objects over Washington State, where his description of their movement was later interpreted by the media through a saucer-like comparison. The resulting image quickly became attached to extraterrestrial technology in newspapers, illustrations, films and advertising.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-and-alien-invasions-in-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY TV Nederland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TV Nederland UFOs and Alien Invasions in Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY TV NederlandUFOs and Alien Invasions in Film - Meaning, Hollywood, List &#124; HISTORYApril 9, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2010</span></span></span>

Artists and designers favoured the saucer because its shape communicated advanced technology without needing explanation. Unlike a conventional rocket, which suggested engineering familiar to humans, the hovering disc appeared unfamiliar and difficult to classify. Its smooth surfaces, unusual movement and ability to arrive silently from the sky made it an ideal visual representation of the unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spaceagemuseum.com/flying-saucers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spaceagemuseum.com">[Space Age Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spaceagemuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on spaceagemuseum.com.</span></span></span>

1950s science fiction cinema established many of the visual conventions still associated with UFOs today. In *The Day the Earth Stood Still* (1951), a large spacecraft landing in Washington, DC transformed the UFO from a distant object into a dramatic public event. The film used the spacecraft’s arrival to explore nuclear anxiety, global conflict and the possibility that advanced beings might judge humanity’s behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-and-alien-invasions-in-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY TV Nederland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TV Nederland UFOs and Alien Invasions in Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY TV NederlandUFOs and Alien Invasions in Film - Meaning, Hollywood, List &#124; HISTORYApril 9, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2010</span></span></span>

## From invasion machines to symbols of contact

Early UFO films often presented alien craft as threats. During the Cold War, the unknown object in the sky reflected concerns about invasion, espionage and weapons technology. Films such as *[The War]({{ 'the-war/' | relative_url }}) of the Worlds* (1953) used extraterrestrial attackers to explore fears about overwhelming technological power and humanity’s vulnerability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-and-alien-invasions-in-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY TV Nederland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TV Nederland UFOs and Alien Invasions in Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY TV NederlandUFOs and Alien Invasions in Film - Meaning, Hollywood, List &#124; HISTORYApril 9, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2010</span></span></span>

Over time, filmmakers developed more complex uses for UFO imagery. Instead of appearing only as weapons or symbols of danger, spacecraft became signs of possible communication and discovery. This change is especially visible in *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* (1977), where the UFO is presented through light, sound and scale rather than simply as a hostile machine. The film’s famous mothership sequence uses visual wonder to suggest contact with something beyond ordinary human experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tcm.com/articles/467311/the-essentials-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tcm.com">[Turner Classic Movies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tcm.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turner Classic Movies The Essentials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turner Classic MoviesThe Essentials - Close Encounters of the Third Kind...</span></span></span>

This shift changed the emotional meaning of UFO imagery. A dark object approaching a city often signals danger, while a glowing craft emerging peacefully can suggest hope, curiosity or a spiritual encounter. Science fiction increasingly used UFOs as a way to ask questions about communication, intelligence and whether humanity could recognise forms of life different from its own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tcm.com/articles/467311/the-essentials-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tcm.com">[Turner Classic Movies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tcm.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turner Classic Movies The Essentials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turner Classic MoviesThe Essentials - Close Encounters of the Third Kind...</span></span></span>

## The role of artwork in shaping UFO imagination

Artwork has played a major role in creating and spreading UFO imagery. Before visual effects made large-scale spacecraft possible on screen, magazine covers, paperback illustrations and promotional artwork provided many people with their first images of alien visitors. These illustrations often exaggerated metallic surfaces, dramatic lighting and unusual landscapes to create a sense of cosmic mystery.

The visual language developed by science fiction artists influenced both entertainment and public expectations. A typical UFO illustration might show a circular craft hovering above a landscape, casting a beam of light over humans below. This composition became so familiar that it shaped how audiences interpreted later fictional scenes and even how some people described unexplained sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spaceagemuseum.com/flying-saucers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spaceagemuseum.com">[Space Age Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spaceagemuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on spaceagemuseum.com.</span></span></span>

Historical artwork also demonstrates that strange objects in the sky have long been represented visually, although these earlier images were not created within modern [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}). Museums have examined historical depictions of unusual aerial phenomena, showing that artists in different eras used mysterious objects in the sky to represent religious, supernatural or unexplained events. These traditions provide context for the later science fiction transformation of UFO imagery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/kunstbibliothek/exhibitions/detail/a-ufo-in-1665/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smb.museum">[Staatliche Museen zu Berlin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smb.museum</span><span class="citation-popover-title">a ufo in 1665</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staatliche Museen zu BerlinA UFO in 1665: The Air Battle of StralsundMay 5, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2023</span></span></span>

## How filmmakers create the feeling of a UFO encounter

UFO imagery in film depends not only on spacecraft design but also on presentation. Directors often use scale, lighting, sound and human reactions to make an object feel extraordinary. A spacecraft can appear frightening because it overwhelms familiar environments, or inspiring because it creates a sense of discovery.

Steven Spielberg’s *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* is a major example of this approach. Rather than focusing mainly on military conflict, the film builds suspense through sightings, mysterious lights and a gradual movement towards communication. The design of the UFOs and the use of light effects make the spacecraft feel less like a vehicle and more like an encounter with an unknown presence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tcm.com/articles/467311/the-essentials-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tcm.com">[Turner Classic Movies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tcm.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Turner Classic Movies The Essentials</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Turner Classic MoviesThe Essentials - Close Encounters of the Third Kind...</span></span></span>

Later science fiction expanded this visual vocabulary. Some films moved away from traditional saucers and created more abstract forms of [alien technology]({{ 'alien-technology/' | relative_url }}), reflecting changing ideas about what advanced civilisations might look like. However, the classic hovering object remains powerful because it instantly communicates a meeting between ordinary life and something unexplained.

## Why UFO imagery continues to influence science fiction

UFO imagery remains important because it combines technological speculation with cultural concerns. The spacecraft itself is rarely the entire point; it represents questions about exploration, uncertainty and humanity’s relationship with the unknown. A UFO can symbolise danger, hope, scientific discovery or a challenge to human assumptions depending on the story around it.

The continuing popularity of UFO imagery also shows the close relationship between fiction and public imagination. Films and artwork do not simply copy existing ideas about UFOs; they organise those ideas into memorable visual forms. In return, those images influence how later generations picture alien encounters, spacecraft and possible futures beyond Earth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-and-alien-invasions-in-film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY TV Nederland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TV Nederland UFOs and Alien Invasions in Film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY TV NederlandUFOs and Alien Invasions in Film - Meaning, Hollywood, List &#124; HISTORYApril 9, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 9, 2010</span></span></span>

    });

      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
