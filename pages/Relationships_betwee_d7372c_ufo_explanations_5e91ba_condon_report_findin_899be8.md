## Introduction

The Condon Report, formally titled *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects*, was the largest government-sponsored scientific investigation of [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}) conducted in the United States during the twentieth century. Led by physicist Edward U. Condon at the University of Colorado between 1966 and 1968, the study examined existing UFO records to determine whether they revealed evidence of unknown technology or a subject worthy of further scientific investigation. Its central conclusion was that the accumulated evidence had not produced discoveries of scientific importance and that further large-scale UFO research was unlikely to advance science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/intro.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[Condon Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon ReportCondon Report -NCAS Introduction...</span></span></span>

## The study’s investigation approach

The Condon Report emerged from a period when UFO reports had become a public and political issue in the United States. The U.S. Air Force had already investigated thousands of sightings through Project Blue Book, but pressure grew for an independent scientific review. The University of Colorado project was funded by the Air Force Office of Scientific Research and was intended to evaluate whether UFO reports contained evidence that could contribute to scientific knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/covrletr.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, University of Colorado Submission Letter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, University of Colorado Submission LetterOctober 31, 1968...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 1968</span></span></span>

Rather than attempting to prove or disprove extraterrestrial visitation directly, the study focused on whether UFO reports contained reliable physical evidence of unknown phenomena. Researchers examined witness accounts, photographs, radar information, astronomical possibilities, atmospheric effects, psychological factors and technological explanations. The final report was extensive, containing hundreds of case discussions and technical analyses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/ucnotice.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, University of Colorado Notice</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, University of Colorado Notice...</span></span></span>

The project’s investigators treated UFO reports as scientific data problems: observations had to be assessed against possible conventional explanations before extraordinary interpretations could be considered. This approach reflected a broader scientific principle used in many fields: an unexplained observation is not automatically evidence for a new physical phenomenon. A case may remain unresolved because of missing information, poor measurements or incomplete records rather than because it demonstrates unknown technology.

## Findings from examined cases

The main finding of the Condon Report was that UFO investigations had not produced evidence of technology beyond known human capabilities. Condon’s summary stated that the study had found nothing from two decades of UFO reports that added to scientific knowledge and concluded that further extensive investigation was unlikely to be scientifically productive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

Many individual cases examined by the project were attributed to ordinary causes. Researchers identified explanations involving aircraft, astronomical objects, balloons, atmospheric phenomena, misinterpretations of lights and errors in observation. These findings supported the view that many UFO reports arise from genuine observations that are later incorrectly identified.

However, the report did not classify every investigated case as solved. Some cases remained unexplained because available evidence was insufficient to determine what had been observed. This distinction became central to later debates: an unresolved case was not considered proof of an unknown craft, but it also was not the same as a confirmed conventional explanation. Critics of the report argued that the presence of unresolved cases weakened the simplicity of the final negative conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/condon-report-1968/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Condon Report · USAF–University of Colorado study · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Condon Report · USAF–University of Colorado study · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

The report’s scientific importance therefore rested less on identifying a new phenomenon and more on establishing an evidential threshold. It argued that UFO reports, as then documented, lacked the kind of repeatable measurements, physical samples or controlled observations required for a major scientific discovery.

## Debates over its conclusions

The Condon Report received strong institutional support after publication, including endorsement from a review panel of the National Academy of Sciences, which concluded that the study provided a useful evaluation of the available evidence. The report subsequently influenced the end of official Air Force UFO investigations, with Project Blue Book closing in 1969.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://condon.ncas.org/text/intro.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: condon.ncas.org">[Condon Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">condon.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon ReportCondon Report -NCAS Introduction...</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, the report remained controversial among some scientists and UFO researchers. One major criticism concerned internal disagreements within the project. Critics highlighted a 1966 memorandum by project coordinator Robert Low, later known as the “Low memo”, which appeared to suggest that some project organisers expected a negative conclusion before the investigation was complete. Supporters of the report argued that the final scientific work should be judged by the published evidence rather than by internal disputes over planning and expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/11/26/the-condon-report-a-scientific-study-of-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newspaceeconomy.ca">[New Space Economy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newspaceeconomy.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Space EconomyThe Condon Report: A Scientific Study of UFOs &#124; New Space EconomyNovember 26, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 26, 2025</span></span></span>

Another criticism focused on the difference between Condon’s summary conclusions and some case analyses in the body of the report. Critics argued that certain unresolved cases deserved more attention, while defenders responded that unexplained observations do not automatically indicate revolutionary technology and that the overall evidence still failed to support extraordinary claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uapcodex.org/cases/condon-report-1968/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uapcodex.org">[UAP Codex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uapcodex.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Codex Condon Report · USAF–University of Colorado study · UAP Codex</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Codex Condon Report · USAF–University of Colorado study · UAP Codex</span></span></span>

## Why the Condon Report still shapes UFO science debates

The lasting importance of the Condon Report comes from its role as a boundary-setting document. It did not establish that every UFO report had been explained, but it argued that the available evidence had not demonstrated a scientifically significant unknown phenomenon. This distinction continues to influence later discussions about unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), where researchers often separate “unidentified” from “evidence of new technology”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

Within the wider relationship between UFOs and [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}), the report marked a contrast between imaginative possibilities and scientific standards of evidence. Science fiction often explores advanced spacecraft and extraterrestrial contact as possibilities, while the Condon investigation asked a narrower empirical question: whether real-world observations provided measurable evidence for such claims. Its conclusion was that they did not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

The report remains influential because it illustrates a recurring challenge in evaluating extraordinary claims: scientific investigation must account for both the possibility that observations reveal something unusual and the possibility that ordinary explanations are sufficient. The Condon Report’s central legacy is therefore not simply a verdict on UFOs, but a long-running debate over how science should assess uncertain evidence.

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
