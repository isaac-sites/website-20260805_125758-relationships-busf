## Introduction

The X-Files changed [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}) by turning alien mysteries into stories about evidence, secrecy and institutional trust. Premiering in 1993, the series followed FBI agents Fox Mulder and Dana Scully as they investigated unexplained cases, with Mulder searching for proof of extraterrestrial activity while Scully approached events through scientific scepticism. Its lasting influence came less from convincing audiences that aliens existed than from creating a powerful fictional framework in which missing information, government secrecy and unanswered questions became central features of UFO discussions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shura.shu.ac.uk/31779/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shura.shu.ac.uk">[SHURA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shura.shu.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SHURAThe Legacy of The X-Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sheffield Hallam University Research ArchiveApril 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 19, 2023</span></span></span>

The programme arrived during a period when public interest in UFOs and conspiracy narratives was already established, but it gave those themes a new mainstream language. ## Government secrets and alien investigations on screen

The central innovation of The X-Files was combining the structure of a police investigation with the uncertainty of [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}). Rather than presenting UFO encounters as simple stories of alien visitors, the series focused on incomplete evidence, conflicting testimony and investigations that reached the limits of what could be proven. This approach made secrecy itself part of the mystery.

The show’s long-running “mythology” storyline developed the idea that alien activity was connected to a government conspiracy. Mulder’s investigation into his sister Samantha’s disappearance gradually expanded into a wider narrative involving secret organisations, military interests and attempts to conceal evidence of extraterrestrial contact. The fictional conspiracy, often associated with the Syndicate, became one of television’s most recognisable portrayals of hidden UFO knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://x-files.fandom.com/wiki/Mythology" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: x-files.fandom.com">[X-Files Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">x-files.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">X-Files Wiki Mythology &#124; X-Files Wiki &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">X-Files Wiki Mythology &#124; X-Files Wiki &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

A key figure in this secrecy narrative was the Cigarette Smoking Man, a government operative whose role represented ambiguity rather than simple villainy. He rarely offered a complete explanation of events; instead, his presence suggested that powerful institutions could manipulate information while leaving the public uncertain about what was true. The character became an enduring symbol of the series’ distrust of official narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/think/article/truth-is-out-there/1676C8CC0635D6D4651A99334554C58B" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University Press THE TRUTH IS OUT THERE &#124; Think &#124; Cambridge Core</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University PressTHE TRUTH IS OUT THERE &#124; Think &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

The relationship between Mulder and Scully gave these themes their intellectual tension. Mulder represented openness to extraordinary possibilities, while Scully represented the demand for evidence and scientific verification. Their partnership avoided making either position completely victorious: the series repeatedly showed Mulder uncovering genuine mysteries, but it also showed Scully challenging weak conclusions and searching for more ordinary explanations. This balance helped The X-Files appeal both to UFO enthusiasts and to viewers interested in sceptical investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ew.com/the-x-files-best-episodes-11933395" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ew.com">[EW.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ew.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The piece also notes the enduring appeal and creative reinventions across the series&#x27; lifespan, culminating in fan-favorite entries from...</span></span></span>

## How the series shaped public expectations

The X-Files influenced UFO culture by changing the emotional and narrative expectations surrounding unexplained phenomena. Earlier UFO stories often focused on [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}), encounters or the possibility of alien visitors. The series added a new emphasis on investigation: the idea that the important question was not only “What was seen?” but also “Who knows more than they are admitting?”

This storytelling choice connected UFO themes with broader concerns about institutional trust. Scholars examining the series’ cultural legacy have highlighted its focus on government mistrust, conspiracy, folklore and UFO beliefs as reasons it became a defining cultural reference point of the 1990s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bloomsbury.com/uk/legacy-of-the-xfiles-9781501387630/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bloomsbury.com">[Bloomsbury]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bloomsbury.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Legacy of The X-Files:: James Fenwick: Bloomsbury Academic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Legacy of The X-Files:: James Fenwick: Bloomsbury Academic - BloomsburyDecember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 14, 2023</span></span></span>

The programme also helped popularise a particular visual and narrative style for UFO investigations. Dark government offices, classified files, mysterious informants and unexplained evidence became familiar conventions not only in science fiction but also in later television dramas about hidden truths. The show’s influence can be seen in the growth of mystery-driven genre television that blended investigation with larger conspiratorial storylines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shura.shu.ac.uk/31779/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shura.shu.ac.uk">[SHURA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shura.shu.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SHURAThe Legacy of The X-Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sheffield Hallam University Research ArchiveApril 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 19, 2023</span></span></span>

However, the impact on UFO belief is more complicated than a simple claim that the series made people believe in aliens. Fiction can provide audiences with concepts and imagery for interpreting unusual experiences, but it does not determine individual conclusions. The X-Files often encouraged curiosity rather than certainty: its recurring message was that evidence matters, but that unanswered questions can remain culturally powerful.

The series also reflected a wider shift in how conspiracy narratives entered popular entertainment. By placing conspiracy themes inside a science fiction detective format, The X-Files made them accessible to mainstream audiences who might not normally engage with UFO literature or paranormal investigations. Its influence was therefore cultural as much as ideological: it gave audiences a shared vocabulary for discussing secrecy and uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wsupress.wayne.edu/9780814339428/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wsupress.wayne.edu">[Wayne State University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wsupress.wayne.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">State University Press The X-Files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wayne State University PressThe X-Files...</span></span></span>

## Legacy in modern UFO discussions

The X-Files remains influential because many contemporary UFO debates still revolve around themes the series popularised: government transparency, hidden information and the difficulty of separating genuine unknowns from speculation. Modern discussions of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), the newer term often used for [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}), continue to involve questions about what governments know, what evidence exists and how public trust is maintained.

The show’s legacy is especially visible in the way UFO stories are now often framed. Reports of unusual sightings are frequently discussed alongside questions about official records, military explanations and possible secrecy. While these real-world debates are not evidence that The X-Files predicted events, the series provided a familiar fictional template for thinking about them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.space.com/space-exploration/search-for-life/disclosure-day-if-et-made-contact-how-would-we-handle-the-news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: space.com">[Space]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">space.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Disclosure day: If ET made contact, how would we handle the news?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">President Donald Trump’s directive to release related government files.
