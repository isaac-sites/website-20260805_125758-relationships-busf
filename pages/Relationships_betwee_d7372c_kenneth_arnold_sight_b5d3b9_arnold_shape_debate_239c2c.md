## Introduction

[Kenneth Arnold]({{ 'kenneth-arnold/' | relative_url }})’s sketches are central to a long-running debate over what he actually described during his famous 1947 sighting near Mount Rainier. The drawings show that the objects were not simply the smooth, circular “flying saucers” that later became a standard [science-fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}) image. Arnold’s own notes described objects that appeared thin, elongated and shaped differently from the classic disc seen in films and illustrations. At the same time, his spoken comparisons to “saucers” and “pie plates” helped create the visual language that connected UFO reports with science fiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theufodatabase.com/docs/kenneth-arnold-1947-07-12-kenneth-arnold-letter-and-drawing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO DatabaseLetter and drawing by Arnold to Air Force Intelligence &#124; The UFO DatabaseJuly 12, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 1947</span></span></span>

## Arnold’s reported object shapes

### The July 1947 drawings

Shortly after the sighting, Arnold submitted a detailed report to Army Air Forces intelligence. Included with the report were sketches showing the appearance of the objects from different angles. In the accompanying notes, Arnold wrote that the objects “seemed longer than wide” and that their thickness was about one twentieth of their width. He also indicated that they appeared mirror-bright and that they did not seem to spin as they travelled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theufodatabase.com/docs/kenneth-arnold-1947-07-12-kenneth-arnold-letter-and-drawing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO DatabaseLetter and drawing by Arnold to Air Force Intelligence &#124; The UFO DatabaseJuly 12, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 1947</span></span></span>

These details complicate the later image of a perfectly round flying saucer. Arnold’s drawing presented an object with a thin profile and a distinctive rear shape rather than a simple circular disc. The side view was particularly important because it suggested that the object’s appearance changed depending on its orientation in flight. When viewed edge-on, Arnold said the objects could appear almost like a line; when they tilted and reflected sunlight, he could judge their form more clearly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/470624arnold.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">470624arnold...</span></span></span>

Arnold’s verbal descriptions were also more varied than the later stereotype suggests. In early interviews, he compared the objects to a pie plate cut in half with a raised or triangular rear section. He also described them as “saucer-like” or “disk”-like, terms that reflected attempts to explain an unfamiliar shape using ordinary objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thelivingmoon.com/49ufo_files/03files2/1947_Kenneth_Arnold.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelivingmoon.com">[The Living Moon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelivingmoon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Living Moon Kenneth Arnold</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Living MoonKenneth Arnold - First Flying Saucer Sighting June 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1947</span></span></span>

The evidence therefore points to a description somewhere between several familiar categories: a flattened object, a disc-like form, a crescent or wing-like shape, and something unlike ordinary aircraft of the period. The ambiguity became one reason the report remained open to different interpretations.

## The gap between words and images

The famous phrase “flying saucer” did not come from a precise technical description of Arnold’s sketches. It developed from the way his comparisons were reported in the press. Arnold had used the image of a saucer mainly when describing the objects’ movement, saying they moved like a saucer skipping across water. Newspapers transformed that comparison into a description of the objects themselves, helping create a phrase that spread internationally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/kenneth-arnold" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kenneth Arnold</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kenneth Arnold - UFO, 1947 &amp; Flying Saucer &#124; HISTORYFebruary 22, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2010</span></span></span>

This distinction mattered because the new phrase immediately created a ready-made visual form. Once “flying saucer” entered popular culture, artists, magazine publishers and filmmakers could represent UFOs as smooth, circular spacecraft. That design became closely associated with [alien technology]({{ 'alien-technology/' | relative_url }}) in science fiction, even though Arnold’s own sketches showed a more unusual and less standardised form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/1947-year-flying-saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[Smithsonian Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1947 year flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Air and Space Museum1947: Year of the Flying Saucer &#124; National Air and Space MuseumJune 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 24, 2022</span></span></span>

The shift illustrates how [UFO imagery]({{ 'ufo-imagery/' | relative_url }}) was shaped by communication as much as by eyewitness reports. A witness might describe movement, angle, brightness and approximate shape, while audiences often preferred a simple visual symbol. The saucer became memorable because it was easy to draw and immediately recognisable, allowing science-fiction creators to adopt it as the default appearance of extraterrestrial machines.

Arnold himself continued to distinguish between what he saw and the public image that followed. His later comments reflected frustration that the phrase “flying saucer” had become a fixed idea that did not fully capture his original account. The debate was therefore not only about the object’s possible identity, but also about whether the public had preserved or transformed his description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/1947-year-flying-saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[Smithsonian Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1947 year flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Air and Space Museum1947: Year of the Flying Saucer &#124; National Air and Space MuseumJune 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 24, 2022</span></span></span>

## Later debates over the sighting details

### Was Arnold’s object really a “saucer”?

The main disagreement among later researchers concerns how much the original report should be considered a saucer description. One interpretation argues that Arnold clearly described thin, disc-like objects and used comparisons such as “pie plate” and “saucer-like,” making the later term a reasonable shorthand. Early newspaper accounts also recorded Arnold using similar comparisons when explaining the appearance of the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thelivingmoon.com/49ufo_files/03files2/1947_Kenneth_Arnold.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thelivingmoon.com">[The Living Moon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thelivingmoon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Living Moon Kenneth Arnold</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Living MoonKenneth Arnold - First Flying Saucer Sighting June 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1947</span></span></span>

A competing interpretation emphasises the differences between Arnold’s sketches and the smooth circular spacecraft that became popular afterwards. Supporters of this view argue that the drawings show that the cultural image of [the flying saucer]({{ 'the-flying-saucer/' | relative_url }}) simplified and altered the original report. The important point is not that Arnold described no disc-like qualities, but that his account contained more specific features than the later stereotype retained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theufodatabase.com/docs/kenneth-arnold-1947-07-12-kenneth-arnold-letter-and-drawing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufodatabase.com">[The UFO Database]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufodatabase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO DatabaseLetter and drawing by Arnold to Air Force Intelligence &#124; The UFO DatabaseJuly 12, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 12, 1947</span></span></span>

### Why the sketches became important in UFO history


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The sketches remain significant because they preserve a rare piece of first-hand evidence from the moment when the modern UFO idea was forming. Unlike later artistic depictions influenced by science fiction, Arnold’s drawings were produced close to the event and before the flying saucer image had fully developed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AArnold_AAF_document.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Arnold AAF document.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Arnold AAF document.jpg - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

</div>

For historians of [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}), the drawings show a transition point. Before 1947, science fiction had already imagined advanced aircraft and visitors from other worlds, but Arnold’s report provided a real-world image that could be adapted into a widely shared symbol. The movement from Arnold’s irregular shapes to the familiar saucer design demonstrates how popular culture can reshape eyewitness accounts into lasting icons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/1947-year-flying-saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[Smithsonian Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1947 year flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Air and Space Museum1947: Year of the Flying Saucer &#124; National Air and Space MuseumJune 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 24, 2022</span></span></span>

The lasting debate over Arnold’s sketches is therefore less about choosing between “saucer” and “not saucer” than about understanding how a complex observation became a simple cultural image. His drawings preserve the uncertainty of the original sighting, while the flying saucer became one of science fiction’s most recognisable designs.
