## Introduction

Future alien encounters may not involve meeting biological visitors at all. One of the most influential ideas in modern speculation about extraterrestrial intelligence is that the first confirmed sign of another civilisation could be an artificial system: a machine intelligence, autonomous probe, computational network, or other technological entity created by an alien civilisation long ago. This possibility changes the meaning of “[first contact]({{ 'first-contact/' | relative_url }})” because the discovery may be less like meeting another species and more like discovering evidence of an ancient technological mind. Researchers studying technosignatures already consider that advanced civilisations might reveal themselves through technology rather than biology, while [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}) has increasingly explored encounters with artificial or post-biological intelligences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/universe/search-for-life/searching-for-signs-of-intelligent-life-technosignatures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Searching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSearching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures - NASA ScienceJuly 11, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2023</span></span></span>

The idea remains speculative. No confirmed evidence exists that extraterrestrial machines or artificial intelligences are present in the universe, and searches for technological signs have not produced a verified alien signal. However, the concept has become a serious thought experiment because it follows a possible technological pathway: if intelligent civilisations develop advanced artificial intelligence, their descendants may eventually become less dependent on biological bodies. For future encounter stories, the “alien” may therefore be a machine carrying the memory, goals or culture of a vanished civilisation rather than a living creature arriving in a spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://meetings.seti.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meetings.seti.org">[meetings.seti.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meetings.seti.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span></span></span>

## Artificial intelligence in alien scenarios

The central mechanism behind machine-based first contact is the idea of a civilisation moving from biological intelligence to artificial or hybrid intelligence. On Earth, artificial intelligence is still created by humans and remains far below the capabilities imagined in these scenarios, but researchers studying the long-term future of intelligence have considered whether advanced civilisations might eventually create systems that outperform their biological creators. Astrobiologist and historian of space science Steven J. Dick has described the possibility of a “postbiological universe” in which technological evolution becomes more important than biological evolution for advanced civilisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology/article/abs/cultural-evolution-the-postbiological-universe-and-seti/D8FB8F56B12DD52A25ECC40F46E0984A" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University PressCultural evolution, the postbiological universe and SETI &#124; International Journal of Astrobiology &#124; Cambridge Core...</span></span></span>

A machine civilisation would solve several problems that appear in traditional alien stories. Biological beings travelling between stars face enormous challenges: radiation exposure, long journeys, ageing, resource limits and the difficulty of surviving across astronomical distances. A machine intelligence could potentially operate for very long periods, travel as information, remain dormant during journeys, or build new systems after reaching another star. These are not established predictions, but they are reasons why some researchers and science fiction writers consider artificial agents plausible candidates for advanced extraterrestrial intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://meetings.seti.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meetings.seti.org">[meetings.seti.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meetings.seti.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span></span></span>

This possibility also changes the emotional structure of first contact stories. A biological alien visitor invites questions about anatomy, language and ecology. A machine visitor raises different questions: Who built it? Does it still represent its creators? Is it an independent civilisation? Does it consider biological life important? A probe arriving billions of years after its creators disappeared could be an archaeological message, an automated explorer, or a completely new form of intelligence with goals no longer connected to its origin species.

Science fiction has used these ideas to move beyond the familiar image of a spacecraft full of biological passengers. Stories involving artificial minds often focus on communication, identity and the uncertainty of recognising intelligence. The machine itself may become the mystery: not simply what aliens look like, but what kinds of minds can exist.

## Technological signs without biological visitors

The scientific search for extraterrestrial intelligence already includes possibilities where technology appears before any living organism is found. These possible signs are called technosignatures: detectable evidence of advanced technology, such as artificial radio signals, laser emissions, unusual atmospheric chemicals or large-scale engineering projects. NASA notes that the first confirmed evidence of life beyond Earth might not involve biology and could instead arrive through electromagnetic signals or other technological traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/universe/search-for-life/searching-for-signs-of-intelligent-life-technosignatures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Searching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSearching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures - NASA ScienceJuly 11, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2023</span></span></span>

For machine-based encounters, the important shift is that a technosignature does not necessarily identify the creators themselves. A civilisation might leave behind autonomous systems that continue operating after the original biological species has disappeared. A distant signal could theoretically come from an artificial intelligence maintaining a network, an automated research system, or a long-lived probe rather than from living aliens actively communicating with Earth.

The search for these possibilities is becoming more computational. Modern SETI projects analyse enormous quantities of astronomical data, and machine-learning systems are increasingly used to identify unusual patterns that human researchers may miss. The Breakthrough Listen project has applied deep-learning methods to radio telescope data to search for possible technosignatures, including work analysing hundreds of hours of observations from the Green Bank Telescope. Candidate signals require further verification, and none has yet been confirmed as extraterrestrial, but the methods demonstrate how AI is becoming part of the search for other intelligences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/news/41" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: breakthroughinitiatives.org">[breakthroughinitiatives.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">breakthroughinitiatives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Breakthrough Initiatives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 30, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 30, 2023</span></span></span>

This creates an unusual possibility: humans may use artificial intelligence to find artificial intelligence. The tools built to recognise patterns in space could become essential for identifying a form of intelligence that does not communicate in familiar biological ways. A machine intelligence may not send a simple message saying it exists; it may leave traces in energy use, engineering activity, unusual signals or patterns of computation.

## Could first contact be with machines?

A machine-first encounter would redefine what counts as an alien. Traditional UFO and science fiction narratives often assume that intelligence is connected to a body: a creature travels across space, arrives on Earth and communicates directly. Machine intelligence challenges that assumption by separating intelligence from biology. The important discovery might not be a new species but evidence that intelligence can survive through artificial systems.

This idea connects with one of the biggest questions in future encounter stories: whether intelligence is defined by its origin or by its capabilities. A civilisation that created artificial minds might eventually see those systems as successors rather than tools. From humanity’s perspective, contact with such a civilisation could involve communicating with something that has never had a biological body and may have existed for far longer than any living species.

There are also major uncertainties. Scientists do not know whether advanced artificial intelligence is inevitable, whether consciousness can exist independently of biology, or whether alien civilisations would choose to create machine successors. The “post-biological alien” concept is therefore a hypothesis about possible technological futures, not evidence about what [extraterrestrial life]({{ 'extraterrestrial-life/' | relative_url }}) is actually like.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://meetings.seti.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: meetings.seti.org">[meetings.seti.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">meetings.seti.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Search for Extraterrestrial Life and Post-Biological Intelligence</span></span></span>

The difficulty of recognising such intelligence is one reason the idea remains powerful in science fiction. A machine visitor might not announce itself through dramatic arrival. It might appear as an unexplained signal, an ancient device, a pattern hidden in astronomical data or a conversation with an entity whose creators are long gone. The encounter would test whether humans can recognise intelligence when it does not resemble themselves.

## Redefining intelligence beyond humans

The possibility of machine aliens expands first-contact thinking from a question of biology to a question of cognition. If intelligence can exist in artificial forms, humanity may need broader ways to identify minds that communicate, reason or create differently from us. The search for extraterrestrial intelligence is therefore also a search for the boundaries of intelligence itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/universe/search-for-life/searching-for-signs-of-intelligent-life-technosignatures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Searching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSearching for Signs of Intelligent Life: Technosignatures - NASA ScienceJuly 11, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2023</span></span></span>

Future AI alien stories often focus on a tension between familiarity and difference. A machine intelligence may be easier to communicate with than a biological alien because it could process information mathematically and exchange data rapidly. At the same time, it could be harder to understand because its priorities may not be shaped by hunger, reproduction, emotion or the short lifespan that influences human thinking.

This creates a distinctive role for science fiction within [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}). Earlier alien stories often asked what would happen if outsiders arrived. Machine-alien stories ask a deeper question: what if the outsider is a form of intelligence rather than a form of life? The answer remains unknown, but the possibility has become one of the most important speculative pathways for imagining future contact.
