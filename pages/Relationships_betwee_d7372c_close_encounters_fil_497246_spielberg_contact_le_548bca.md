## Introduction

Steven Spielberg’s *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* (1977) changed the way popular culture imagined alien contact. Instead of presenting extraterrestrials mainly as invaders, enemies, or threats to defeat, the film made [first contact]({{ 'first-contact/' | relative_url }}) a problem of communication, curiosity and mutual recognition. Its lasting influence came from turning the “close encounter” from a frightening mystery into a dramatic meeting between two intelligences trying to understand one another. The film became one of the defining [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}) works about contact with non-human life and helped make the language of close encounters familiar to a global audience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/item/event-421188/close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-1977/2026-06-05/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)* &#124; Library of CongressJune 5, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2026</span></span></span>

Spielberg’s approach did not invent the idea of alien communication, but it transformed its place in mainstream science fiction. By combining UFO themes, scientific investigation, music and human emotion, *Close Encounters* offered a different model of the future: humanity’s first meeting with another civilisation might be an act of discovery rather than a battle. That shift influenced later films, television and public imagination about what an encounter with extraterrestrial intelligence could mean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span>

## The film’s first-contact approach

### Moving alien stories from conflict to encounter

Before *Close Encounters*, many major alien films had focused on invasion, replacement or destruction. Spielberg’s film deliberately moved away from that tradition by making the central question not “How do humans defeat aliens?” but “How do humans respond when something unknown reaches out?” The story follows ordinary people, including electrical worker Roy Neary, whose experiences with UFOs draw them towards a carefully organised attempt at contact. Scientists in the film are not simply preparing weapons or defences; they are trying to understand and communicate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/item/event-421188/close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-1977/2026-06-05/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)* &#124; Library of CongressJune 5, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2026</span></span></span>

This change was important because it connected UFO ideas with a more optimistic branch of science fiction. The film treated the unknown as something that could inspire wonder as well as fear. Its depiction of scientists, civilians and visitors meeting at Devil’s Tower created a visual language for first contact that was based on cooperation rather than immediate conflict. The result was a new cultural reference point for stories about humanity encountering another intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/why-i-love-close-encounters-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bfi.org.uk">[BFI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bfi.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bfi.org.uk.</span></span></span>

The film also gave the close-encounter concept a wider audience. Although the term originated in astronomer J. Allen Hynek’s classification of [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}), Spielberg’s film became the version most people encountered through popular culture. The movie helped attach the idea of a “close encounter” not only to UFO [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) but to the broader possibility of direct communication with an alien civilisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/item/event-421188/close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-1977/2026-06-05/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)* &#124; Library of CongressJune 5, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2026</span></span></span>

## Communication instead of invasion themes

The most influential idea in *Close Encounters* is that contact begins with understanding. Spielberg represents communication as a process of decoding signals rather than exchanging threats. The film’s famous use of a five-note musical sequence turns language into something universal: humans and aliens do not begin with shared words, but with patterns, sounds and a willingness to respond.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span>

This approach reflected a broader shift in science fiction during the late twentieth century. Instead of treating extraterrestrials only as symbols of danger, *Close Encounters* presented them as a test of humanity’s ability to cooperate and think beyond its own assumptions. The alien visitors are mysterious, but the film’s emotional centre is human openness to something beyond Earth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/why-i-love-close-encounters-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bfi.org.uk">[BFI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bfi.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bfi.org.uk.</span></span></span>

The communication theme also influenced how later fictional first contacts were written. Many subsequent stories explored questions that *Close Encounters* helped popularise: How would humans recognise intelligence that is unlike our own? Could art, mathematics or shared patterns become a bridge between species? Would contact challenge political systems, scientific beliefs or ideas about humanity’s place in the universe?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/37761/chapter-abstract/332135354" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicClose Encounters: Cultural Impact &#124; Close Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Liverpool Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford Academic...</span></span></span>

The film’s optimistic vision did not eliminate darker alien narratives. Science fiction continued to explore invasion and conflict, but *Close Encounters* expanded the genre’s possibilities by making peaceful contact equally dramatic. It established that a story about aliens could be built around dialogue, discovery and emotional transformation rather than only survival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span>

## Its influence on later science fiction

### A new model for alien meetings

The influence of *Close Encounters* can be seen in later science fiction that treats alien contact as a question of communication. Films such as *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial* continued Spielberg’s interest in sympathetic alien encounters, while later works including *Arrival* explored the challenge of understanding a genuinely unfamiliar form of intelligence. The legacy is not that all later stories copied Spielberg’s style, but that they inherited his central question: what happens when contact requires patience and interpretation rather than violence?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ew.com/movies/best-alien-movies-of-all-time/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ew.com">[EW.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ew.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The 40 best alien movies of all time</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Each listed movie is highlighted for its unique take on the theme of extraterrestrial life and includes some classic films as well as mor...</span></span></span>

The film also affected the cultural imagination beyond cinema. Researchers and commentators have noted that its vision of extraterrestrial contact contributed to continuing public interest in [UFO culture]({{ 'ufo-culture/' | relative_url }}), SETI (the search for extraterrestrial intelligence) and discussions about humanity’s possible place among other civilisations. Its influence came partly from presenting alien contact as a meaningful human experience rather than merely a spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/37761/chapter-abstract/332135354" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicClose Encounters: Cultural Impact &#124; Close Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Liverpool Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford Academic...</span></span></span>

### Why the film remains a reference point

More than four decades after release, *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* remains significant because it changed the emotional vocabulary of [alien contact stories]({{ 'alien-contact-stories/' | relative_url }}). The film suggested that meeting an unknown civilisation would not only be a scientific event but also a cultural and philosophical one. Its enduring image is not an attack from the stars, but a moment when two different forms of life attempt to understand each other.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/item/event-421188/close-encounters-of-the-third-kind-1977/2026-06-05/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressClose Encounters of the Third Kind (1977)* &#124; Library of CongressJune 5, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2026</span></span></span>

The film’s legacy lies in making communication itself the dramatic event. By presenting first contact as an exchange of signals, ideas and trust, Spielberg helped move science fiction beyond the simple question of whether aliens might arrive. He encouraged audiences to consider a more complex possibility: if contact ever occurred, the greatest challenge might not be survival, but learning how to listen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span>
