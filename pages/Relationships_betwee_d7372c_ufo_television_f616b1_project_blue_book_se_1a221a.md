## Introduction

The television series *Project Blue Book* revived one of the most recognisable episodes in UFO history by turning a real United States Air Force investigation programme into a Cold War-era [science-fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}) drama. Running on the History channel from 2019 to 2020, the series used the work of astronomer Dr J. Allen Hynek and the Air Force’s investigations of unexplained [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) as the foundation for a story about evidence, secrecy and the limits of scientific explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/project_blue_book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rottentomatoes.com">[Rotten Tomatoes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rottentomatoes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rotten Tomatoes Project Blue Book &#124; Rotten Tomatoes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rotten TomatoesProject Blue Book &#124; Rotten TomatoesJanuary 8, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 2019</span></span></span>

## Historical investigation as entertainment

### Turning Air Force files into a television mystery

The original Project Blue Book was a genuine US Air Force programme that investigated reports of unidentified flying objects between 1952 and 1969. The programme collected 12,618 reports, with most eventually classified as explained phenomena such as aircraft, astronomical objects or weather-related events. A smaller number remained unidentified because available information was insufficient to provide a firm explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The television adaptation takes this historical framework and reshapes it into a character-driven investigation drama. Its central figure, Dr J. Allen [Hynek]({{ 'hynek/' | relative_url }}), was based on a real astronomer who served as a scientific consultant to the Air Force’s UFO investigations. Hynek initially approached [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}) as a problem requiring conventional explanations, but his experiences gradually led him to argue that some cases deserved more serious scientific attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/5492638/project-blue-book-tv-show-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens and Cold War Paranoia Collide in Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force&#x27;s real-life investigation of UFO sightings during the post-World War II era, when tensions with the Soviet Union were high. J...</span></span></span>

This evolution made Hynek a particularly suitable figure for television. His story already contained a dramatic arc: a respected scientist enters a controversial field expecting to explain away unusual reports, then becomes increasingly interested in the unresolved questions surrounding them. The series uses this transformation as its main narrative engine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/5492638/project-blue-book-tv-show-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens and Cold War Paranoia Collide in Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force&#x27;s real-life investigation of UFO sightings during the post-World War II era, when tensions with the Soviet Union were high. The show also introduced fictional elements to strengthen the drama. Captain Michael Quinn, played by Michael Malarkey, serves as Hynek’s military partner, but the character was created for the series rather than being a direct historical figure. Critics noted that while Hynek and many of the investigated cases were connected to real history, the relationship between the investigators and the events depicted were significantly adapted for entertainment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/project-blue-book-review-spoiler-free/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: denofgeek.com">[Den of Geek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">denofgeek.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Den of Geek Project Blue Book Review (Spoiler Free) &#124; Den of Geek</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Den of Geek Project Blue Book Review (Spoiler Free) &#124; Den of Geek</span></span></span>

## Science versus unexplained events

The strongest connection between *Project Blue Book* and science-fiction storytelling is its focus on uncertainty. The series does not rely only on the idea of alien visitors; instead, it asks how scientific methods respond when evidence is incomplete, inconsistent or difficult to interpret.

Hynek’s real role reflected this tension. He was brought into UFO investigations because the Air Force wanted scientific expertise, but his later views challenged the assumption that every unusual sighting could quickly be dismissed. His development became one of the reasons he remained an important figure in UFO history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popularmechanics.com/military/a70995826/j-allen-hynek-project-blue-book-ufo-investigation-truth/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popularmechanics.com">[Popular Mechanics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popularmechanics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Allen Hynek from a government consultant and UFO skeptic into the foremost advocate for serious scientific study of unidentified flying o...</span></span></span>

The programme’s historical setting also gives its fictional conflicts greater weight. Project Blue Book operated during the Cold War, when military secrecy, fears of technological competition and concerns about national security shaped public reactions to unusual aerial reports. Television writers used this atmosphere to explore a familiar science-fiction theme: whether institutions can be trusted when dealing with unknown phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/5492638/project-blue-book-tv-show-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens and Cold War Paranoia Collide in Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force&#x27;s real-life investigation of UFO sightings during the post-World War II era, when tensions with the Soviet Union were high. J...</span></span></span>

However, the series separates historical evidence from dramatic interpretation. The real Air Force investigation did not conclude that UFO sightings proved extraterrestrial technology. Official records preserved by the National Archives show that Project Blue Book closed in 1969, with the Air Force reporting that it had found no evidence that investigated sightings represented extraterrestrial vehicles or technology beyond contemporary scientific knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This difference between the historical record and the fictional version is central to understanding the show. *Project Blue Book* is not a documentary reconstruction of the investigation; it is a science-fiction drama that uses historical material to examine questions about evidence, belief and institutional power.

## Fictional additions and audience appeal

The appeal of *Project Blue Book* comes from combining real archives with the storytelling techniques of modern mystery television. Like earlier UFO dramas, including *[The X-Files]({{ 'the-x-files/' | relative_url }})*, it uses investigations as a way to explore competing interpretations of strange events. The audience is encouraged to consider several possibilities at once: mistaken identity, scientific misunderstanding, secret technology or something genuinely unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/5492638/project-blue-book-tv-show-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens and Cold War Paranoia Collide in Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force&#x27;s real-life investigation of UFO sightings during the post-World War II era, when tensions with the Soviet Union were high. Hynek’s reputation gives the drama a connection to real UFO research, while his changing views provide a human story about how evidence can challenge existing assumptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/project-blue-book-review-spoiler-free/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: denofgeek.com">[Den of Geek]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">denofgeek.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Den of Geek Project Blue Book Review (Spoiler Free) &#124; Den of Geek</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Den of Geek Project Blue Book Review (Spoiler Free) &#124; Den of Geek</span></span></span>

Its fictional additions create the larger-scale suspense expected from contemporary television. Secret meetings, military pressure and expanded conspiratorial elements heighten the drama, even where they go beyond what historical records establish. This approach reflects a common pattern in UFO fiction: real investigations provide the foundation, while science-fiction adds emotional stakes and narrative uncertainty.

The result is a hybrid form of UFO storytelling. *Project Blue Book* does not simply recreate an investigation programme; it transforms an archive of reports into a story about how societies respond to the unknown.
