## Introduction

Film-makers create convincing UFO encounters not only by designing unusual spacecraft, but by controlling how those objects are seen. Lighting determines whether a UFO feels like a threat, a mystery, a scientific discovery or a moment of wonder. Through darkness, beams of light, colour, scale and contrast, directors can make a simple shape in the sky appear beyond human understanding. The most influential UFO scenes often show less than they reveal: the audience sees the effects of an unknown presence before seeing the object itself.

This approach is central to films such as *Close Encounters of the [Third Kind]({{ 'third-kind/' | relative_url }})* (1977), where glowing spacecraft are presented as phenomena of light rather than ordinary machines. Visual effects supervisor Douglas Trumbull and cinematographer Vilmos Zsigmond used controlled illumination, haze and layered exposures to create UFOs defined by brilliance and movement rather than mechanical detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span> Later [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}) films continued this idea by using lighting to shape emotional responses, from fear and uncertainty to communication and awe. The atmosphere of a UFO encounter is therefore built as much through presentation as through the spacecraft design itself.

## Light and scale techniques

### Why UFOs are often shown through light first

A major technique in UFO cinema is delaying a clear view of the craft. Instead of presenting a detailed vehicle immediately, filmmakers often introduce an unnatural glow, moving reflections, silhouettes or changing shadows. This creates a gap between what characters experience and what they understand, allowing the audience to share their uncertainty.

*Close Encounters of the Third Kind* is one of the clearest examples of this method. The film’s UFOs are associated with intense, multicoloured illumination that suggests speed, energy and technology beyond human capability. Trumbull described the visual goal as creating an impression of “motion, velocity, luminosity and brilliance”, using techniques that suggested shapes through light rather than relying only on hard physical outlines. The production combined illuminated models, smoke effects, optical techniques and multiple exposures to make the spacecraft appear almost impossible to define.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://catalog.afi.com/Film/67160-CLOSE-ENCOUNTERS-OF-THE-THIRD-KIND" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: catalog.afi.com">[AFI Catalog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">catalog.afi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Catalog AFI&#124;Catalog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AFI CatalogAFI&#124;Catalog...</span></span></span>

This choice changed the meaning of the UFO image. A metallic spacecraft with visible machinery suggests engineering and construction, while a glowing object surrounded by darkness feels less like a vehicle and more like an encounter with the unknown. The viewer is not simply looking at a machine; they are witnessing an event.

### Darkness makes the unknown larger

Night-time settings are particularly effective because they remove ordinary visual references. A UFO appearing above a dark landscape, empty road or isolated house can seem enormous because the audience has fewer familiar objects with which to judge its distance and size.

In *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, the final appearance of the mothership uses darkness and contrast to create a sense of overwhelming scale. The craft’s lights dominate the frame, while the surrounding landscape becomes secondary. The result is that the audience experiences the arrival through human reactions rather than through technical inspection. The mothership’s design itself was developed around luminosity, with the production deliberately avoiding a purely mechanical appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikizero.org/wiki/en/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikizero.org">[WikiZero]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikizero.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Zero Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind - WikiZeroNovember 16, 1977...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 1977</span></span></span>

Modern films use similar principles in different ways. In *Arrival* (2016), the alien spacecraft is a huge, smooth object that contrasts with the landscape around it. Cinematographer Bradford Young used controlled lighting inside the ship to create a space that felt both alien and emotionally intimate. Rather than filling the spacecraft with visible futuristic technology, the production used a large, soft light source behind a screen, making the encounter feel mysterious and almost ceremonial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theasc.com/articles/arrival-cinematography-bradford-young" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theasc.com">[The American Society of Cinematographers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theasc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The American Society of CinematographersBradford Young, ASC Shoots Denis Villeneuve&#x27;s &#x27;Arrival&#x27;April 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 29, 2026</span></span></span>

### Colour changes the meaning of contact

Colour is one of the fastest ways for a film to communicate the emotional meaning of a UFO. Bright white light can suggest purity, discovery or overwhelming intelligence. Red and harsh shadows can suggest danger. Cold blue tones can make an encounter feel distant, clinical or unfamiliar.

The choice of colour also separates human spaces from alien ones. In *Arrival*, the contrast between the everyday world and the spacecraft environment helps express the difference between human expectations and the unknown visitors. The ship’s interior avoids the typical brightly lit “future technology” style and instead uses controlled darkness and soft illumination to create a reflective atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/bradford-young-asc-arrival/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishcinematographer.co.uk">[British Cinematographer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishcinematographer.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Cinematographer Bradford Young ASC / Arrival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British CinematographerBradford Young ASC / Arrival - British CinematographerNovember 15, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The important point is that lighting does not merely decorate the UFO. It establishes the audience’s interpretation of what the UFO represents.

## Sound and human reactions

Although lighting creates the first impression of a UFO encounter, filmmakers often combine it with sound and human responses to make the moment believable. A silent, glowing object can feel unnatural, but the reactions of people on screen provide emotional scale.

In *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, the encounter sequence connects visual effects with communication. The spacecraft’s lights are not presented only as decoration; they become part of a visual language between humans and the visitors. The famous final sequence combines light patterns, musical signals and human observation to suggest an attempt at understanding rather than a simple invasion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/877-close-encounters-of-the-third-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: criterion.com">[The Criterion Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">criterion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Criterion CollectionClose Encounters of the Third Kind &#124; Current &#124; The Criterion CollectionNovember 12, 1990...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 12, 1990</span></span></span>

Human reactions also help audiences measure the impossible. A person shielding their eyes from an intense light, standing in silhouette against a massive glow or approaching a strange object cautiously makes the scale of the encounter easier to imagine. The viewer understands the UFO through the characters’ physical responses.

This approach separates UFO scenes from ordinary action sequences. A spaceship battle may focus on movement and destruction, but a [first-contact]({{ 'first-contact/' | relative_url }}) scene depends on anticipation. The pause before understanding often becomes the most important part of the experience.

## Building mystery through presentation

### Showing effects instead of explanations

One of the most effective UFO filmmaking strategies is to focus on consequences rather than complete explanations. A strange light crossing a room, a beam passing through fog or a landscape suddenly illuminated can create a stronger sense of mystery than a detailed spacecraft reveal.

This method reflects a broader principle in science fiction cinema: the unknown is often more powerful when partially hidden. A UFO that is completely explained can become ordinary, while one that remains visually ambiguous continues to invite interpretation.

The same idea appears in films that move away from traditional flying saucers. *Arrival* does not present its spacecraft as a collection of visible mechanisms. Instead, the film uses atmosphere, scale and controlled lighting to make the object feel like a place where normal human perception does not fully apply.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/4677598/arrival-oscar-cinematography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the Cinematography of the Oscar-Nominated Movie Arrival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The film, directed by Denis Villeneuve, emphasizes a journey from darkness to light, mirroring the protagonist Louise Banks&#x27; personal enl...</span></span></span>

### The tension between realism and spectacle

UFO lighting also involves a creative tension. Too much spectacle can make an encounter feel artificial, while too much realism can reduce the sense of wonder. Successful films balance familiar environments with impossible events.

*Close Encounters of the Third Kind* places extraordinary lights over ordinary locations such as roads, homes and rural landscapes. This contrast makes the supernatural feel closer to everyday life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_Encounters_of_the_Third_Kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Close Encounters of the Third Kind</span></span></span> The viewer recognises the setting, which makes the unexplained object seem more convincing.

By contrast, *Arrival* uses a more restrained approach. Its lighting avoids the colourful spectacle associated with classic [flying saucer imagery]({{ 'flying-saucer-imagery/' | relative_url }}) and instead creates uncertainty through shadows, scale and limited visibility. The result is a different kind of realism: not “this looks like a real machine”, but “this feels like a real human response to something impossible”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://britishcinematographer.co.uk/bradford-young-asc-arrival/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishcinematographer.co.uk">[British Cinematographer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishcinematographer.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Cinematographer Bradford Young ASC / Arrival</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British CinematographerBradford Young ASC / Arrival - British CinematographerNovember 15, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why lighting remains central to UFO imagery

Film lighting has become one of the defining tools for creating UFO encounter atmosphere because it controls the relationship between the known and the unknown. The spacecraft design tells audiences what an object looks like, but lighting tells them what it means.

A UFO can appear frightening when it cuts through darkness with aggressive beams, mysterious when it remains partly hidden, or awe-inspiring when light transforms the landscape around it. Across different eras of science fiction, directors have repeatedly used these techniques to turn artificial effects into emotional experiences.

The strongest UFO scenes are therefore not remembered only because of the craft on screen. They are remembered because lighting makes viewers feel the same uncertainty, curiosity and wonder experienced by the characters witnessing something beyond their understanding.

    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
