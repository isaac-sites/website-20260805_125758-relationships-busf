## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), the Pentagon office responsible for investigating unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP), has reviewed some of the most persistent claims that governments possess recovered alien spacecraft or have secretly reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology. Its central finding was that it found no verified evidence of extraterrestrial hardware, recovered “off-world” craft, or confirmed UAP cases representing [alien technology]({{ 'alien-technology/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

## Reviewing government records

AARO’s Historical Record Report, Volume I, examined US government involvement with UAP from 1945 onwards, including previous investigations, alleged crash-retrieval stories, claims of hidden programmes and accusations that recovered materials had been analysed for reverse engineering. The review was created after Congress directed the Department of Defense to examine the historical record of UAP-related activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/events/aaro-historical-record-vol" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Disclosure ArchivesAARO releases Historical Record Report, Volume I · Disclosure ArchivesMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

The report’s main finding was that no US government investigation, academic study or official review had confirmed a UAP sighting as extraterrestrial technology. AARO also stated that it found no empirical evidence supporting claims that the US government or private companies had recovered and exploited alien spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

A key part of the review involved separating genuine classified aerospace activity from claims of alien technology. AARO found that some historical UAP stories were likely influenced by real but secret programmes, where advanced aircraft or surveillance projects were mistaken for something beyond human capability. The report argued that secrecy surrounding [military technology]({{ 'military-technology/' | relative_url }}) created conditions where speculation could grow around unexplained [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

The review also examined allegations of hidden reverse-engineering efforts. According to AARO, several claimed programmes were either not real, were misunderstood versions of legitimate defence projects, or involved proposals that never became operational programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

## Claims versus verified evidence

The strongest claims about alien technology generally involve three ideas: recovered spacecraft, unusual materials, and secret engineering programmes. AARO’s review tested these claims against the type of evidence scientists would normally require: documented provenance, independent analysis, repeatable measurements and clear links between a material or object and a non-human origin.

One frequently discussed example involved a metal sample claimed by some investigators to have come from an extraterrestrial craft. AARO examined the material and concluded that it was a manufactured, terrestrial alloy with ordinary characteristics rather than a substance showing evidence of non-human manufacture. The office identified the sample as primarily consisting of magnesium, zinc and bismuth, with other trace elements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important because unusual appearance is not the same as unusual origin. Materials science often finds unexpected structures, unusual manufacturing techniques or uncommon alloys created by human industry. To demonstrate alien technology, researchers would need evidence showing that a material could not reasonably have been produced by human processes and that it was connected to an extraterrestrial source. AARO concluded that the reviewed materials did not meet that threshold.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

Claims of recovered craft face a similar evidence problem. A report from a witness, even if made in good faith, does not by itself establish that an object was extraterrestrial. Scientific evaluation requires physical access, reliable records of where an object came from, and independent verification. NASA’s UAP study reached a related conclusion: the limited number of high-quality observations currently available makes firm scientific conclusions about UAP origins impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

The broader scientific issue is that unidentified does not automatically mean unknown technology. An object can remain unidentified because of incomplete sensor data, poor viewing conditions, missing context or limited information. NASA has noted that many UAP reports lack the detailed measurements needed for rigorous analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP FAQs - NASA ScienceMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why unresolved cases remain open

AARO’s finding of no confirmed alien technology does not eliminate all unresolved UAP reports. Some cases remain open because investigators do not yet have enough information to determine what occurred. The remaining uncertainty usually reflects limitations in data rather than positive evidence for extraterrestrial engineering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP FAQs - NASA ScienceMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

The difference between “unresolved” and “extraterrestrial” is central to scientific evaluation. A radar track, pilot observation or video recording may show something unusual, but determining whether it represents a new technology requires additional evidence. Investigators must consider possibilities such as sensor limitations, atmospheric effects, ordinary aircraft, balloons, drones or classified human systems before concluding that a phenomenon represents something fundamentally new.

AARO’s later reporting continued this evidence-based approach. Its annual UAP reviews have found that resolved cases generally correspond to ordinary objects and phenomena, including balloons, satellites, aircraft and unmanned systems, while unresolved cases often remain open because available data is insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/aaro-fy24-consolidated-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Paradigm Instituteaaro fy24 consolidated report – New Paradigm Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>

This approach places AARO’s work within the wider scientific method: extraordinary claims require evidence that can distinguish them from more ordinary explanations. In the context of [UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}) and [science fiction]({{ 'science-fiction/' | relative_url }}), the idea of recovered alien technology remains a powerful cultural theme, but AARO’s review found that current government records do not provide verified evidence that such technology exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 1March 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2024</span></span></span>

The unresolved nature of some UAP cases keeps scientific investigation worthwhile, but the evidence currently supports caution rather than a conclusion that humanity has acquired extraterrestrial machines. The main challenge for future research is therefore improving the quality of observations, measurements and physical evidence rather than assuming an alien explanation for unexplained events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>
