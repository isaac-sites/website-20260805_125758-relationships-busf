## Introduction

[UFO reports]({{ 'ufo-reports/' | relative_url }}) often begin with a real observation but become difficult to explain because the human brain must interpret incomplete information very quickly. A witness can accurately describe what they experienced — a bright light, an unusual shape, a sudden movement — while still reaching an incorrect conclusion about the object’s distance, speed or cause. This distinction between seeing and identifying is central to [scientific explanations]({{ 'scientific-explanations/' | relative_url }}) of UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s6chap01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Sec VI, Chapter 1 -- Perceptual Problems</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Sec VI, Chapter 1 -- Perceptual Problems...</span></span></span>

Human perception is not a camera that records the world without interpretation. The visual system combines incoming signals with assumptions about size, motion, distance and context. When a sighting occurs at night, in poor visibility, without reference points, or during a brief encounter, the brain has fewer clues to construct an accurate explanation. NASA’s review of unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) emphasised that many reports lack the high-quality data needed for firm conclusions, highlighting the importance of better observations and measurements rather than relying only on human accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Limits of visual perception

The main difficulty in many UFO [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) is not whether a person saw something, but whether the available visual information was sufficient to identify it. Human vision evolved to interpret familiar objects in ordinary environments, where distance, scale and movement are usually supported by surrounding features. A small object close to the observer and a large object far away can produce similar images on the retina, leaving the brain to estimate size and distance using context.

The problem becomes greater when an object appears against an empty sky. Without buildings, trees or terrain for comparison, observers have limited ability to judge how far away something is. The classic psychological analysis of UFO reports in the [Condon Report]({{ 'condon-report/' | relative_url }}) described perception as an interpretive process in which the observer assigns characteristics such as distance, direction, shape and colour even when the visual information does not uniquely determine those properties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s6chap01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Sec VI, Chapter 1 -- Perceptual Problems</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Sec VI, Chapter 1 -- Perceptual Problems...</span></span></span>

### Why a clear sighting can still be misidentified

A common misunderstanding is that a confident witness must have made an accurate identification. In reality, confidence and correctness are separate issues. A person may honestly report a striking event while being mistaken about what produced it.

Several normal visual processes contribute to this:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Limited reference points:** A light moving across a dark sky can appear to perform unusual manoeuvres because the observer cannot easily judge its true path or distance.
* **Expectation effects:** The brain uses previous knowledge to interpret ambiguous shapes. If an object does not immediately match a familiar category, the mind may create a more unusual explanation.
* **Attention limits:** People cannot process every detail of a rapidly changing scene. The brain fills gaps using assumptions and patterns.

</div>

The Condon Report’s psychological review divided possible reporting errors into broad categories, including misidentification of real stimuli, perception of unreal stimuli as real, and deliberate falsification. Importantly, the researchers did not treat all witnesses as unreliable; instead, they noted that normal perceptual processes can create mistakes even among sincere observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s6chap03.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Sec VI, Chap 3: Psychological Aspects of UFO Reports...</span></span></span>

## Distance and motion illusions

Many dramatic UFO descriptions involve extraordinary speed, hovering, sudden turns or impossible acceleration. However, judging motion in the sky is particularly difficult because the observer often lacks the information needed to calculate true movement.

### The difficulty of judging speed in the sky

An object’s apparent movement depends on both its actual motion and its distance from the observer. A nearby object moving slowly can appear to move rapidly across the field of view, while a distant aircraft travelling at high speed may appear almost stationary.

This is especially relevant for pilots and trained observers. Aviation experience improves recognition of many aircraft-related phenomena, but it does not eliminate the basic limits of human perception. Without accurate distance measurements, even experienced observers can misjudge an object’s trajectory.

The same principle applies to apparent hovering. An aircraft approaching directly towards an observer may show little sideways movement, making it appear stationary until it changes direction. Balloons and other objects carried by wind layers can also appear to make unexpected movements because their motion is not always aligned with the direction a viewer expects. Scientific investigations of UFO reports have repeatedly identified aircraft, balloons, satellites and atmospheric effects as important categories requiring careful analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

### The role of optical conditions

Light itself can create misleading impressions. Bright objects near the horizon may appear distorted because atmospheric conditions affect how light travels through the air. Reflections, glare and camera limitations can also transform ordinary objects into unusual shapes.

These issues become particularly important with photographs and videos. A recording may appear to provide objective evidence, but cameras have their own limitations: lens flare, focus problems, digital processing and lack of depth information can all change how an object appears. NASA’s UAP study therefore recommended improving data quality, sensor calibration and the recording of technical information alongside observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Memory after unusual sightings

A UFO report is rarely just a record of the first few seconds of observation. Most accounts are created after the event, when memory, discussion and interpretation have already influenced the original experience.

Human memory is reconstructive rather than a perfect recording. When people recall an unusual event, they may unintentionally organise incomplete details into a coherent story. Later conversations, questions from investigators or exposure to other interpretations can influence how the memory is described.

Research on eyewitness testimony has shown that confidence is not always a reliable indicator of accuracy, particularly when memories have been influenced by outside information or repeated retelling. Studies of eyewitness identification have found that memory can be altered by questioning methods and later information, demonstrating why unusual observations must be evaluated alongside independent evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10932793/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Practice and feedback effects on the confidence-accuracy relation in eyewitness memory - PubMed...</span></span></span>

### Why unusual events become stronger memories

Strange experiences are often remembered vividly because they attract attention and emotional significance. A person who sees an unexplained light may spend hours or years thinking about the event, searching for explanations and comparing it with similar reports. This continued focus can strengthen certain details while other details become less certain.

For UFO investigations, this creates a careful balance. A witness account remains valuable because it provides information about what was observed. However, the scientific question is not only “what did the witness experience?” but also “which parts of that experience can be independently verified?”

## The scientific value of understanding perception errors

Understanding perception errors does not mean dismissing UFO witnesses. Scientific investigation separates two questions that are often confused: whether someone genuinely experienced something unusual, and whether the explanation they assigned to it is correct.

Historical investigations such as the US Air Force’s Project Blue Book examined thousands of UFO reports and found that many could be attributed to known objects or phenomena, while some remained unresolved because the available information was insufficient. The project recorded 12,618 reports between 1947 and 1969, with 701 classified as unidentified, but it found no evidence that these cases represented technology beyond known science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

Modern UAP research follows a similar principle. The challenge is not simply finding unusual observations; it is collecting enough reliable information — multiple measurements, calibrated sensors and detailed context — to distinguish genuine unknowns from situations where human perception has been pushed beyond its normal limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

Perception errors are therefore not a minor detail in UFO research. They are a central mechanism explaining how ordinary events can become extraordinary reports. The gap between observation and interpretation is where many UFO mysteries begin.
